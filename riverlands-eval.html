Riverlands Youth Connect: end-of-program evaluation

A 12-month mentoring program for young people aged 14 to 17 across three Riverina sites, run from February to December 2026. This short report covers outcomes for completing participants and what shaped them.

142young people enrolled
118completed (83%)
31interviews (participants, mentors, parents)
3sites
Summary

Completing participants reported higher connectedness and wellbeing at the end of the program than at the start, with an average gain of 0.57 on the five-point connectedness scale. The gains were similar in Wagga Wagga and Albury and noticeably smaller in Griffith. Interviews point to mentor continuity as the most likely explanation for that difference, although the design cannot rule out other site factors.

KEQ 2 · What helped and hindered

Findings

A consistent mentor mattered more than session frequency

Observed
Griffith had three mentor changes during the year; the other sites had none. Griffith attendance (71%) was close to the other sites (78% and 80%), but its connectedness gain was about half.
Interpretation
Participants and parents at Griffith described having to "start again" with new mentors. This fits the pattern in the scores, though Griffith also differs in other ways (more participants travelling from outlying farms).
Judgement
Mentor retention is likely a key condition for the program to work. Moderate evidence
Show evidence base
  • Program records: mentor rosters by site, Feb–Dec
  • Pre/post survey, n=37 Griffith completers
  • 9 of 11 Griffith interviewees raised mentor change unprompted

Practical activities opened the door to talking

Observed
Sessions built around a task (cooking, bike repair, job applications) were mentioned in 19 of 24 participant interviews as the ones they valued most.
Interpretation
Young people described these as lower pressure than "sit-down chats", and mentors said harder conversations often came up while working on something.
Judgement
Task-based sessions appear to support engagement. Strong evidence
Show evidence base
  • 24 participant interviews across all sites
  • Mentor reflection logs (412 sessions)
  • Consistent across sites and age groups

Transport limited who could take part

Observed
Of the 24 participants who did not complete, 10 gave transport as a reason in exit calls. Most of these lived more than 20 km from the venue.
Interpretation
The program may be reaching town-based young people more reliably than those in outlying areas.
Judgement
Plausible but not established. Exit call coverage was incomplete. Limited evidence

KEQ 3 · Worth continuing?

Overall judgement

On balance, the evidence supports continuing the program. The outcome gains are modest but consistent at two of the three sites, and the main weakness identified (mentor turnover) is something the program can act on. The case would be stronger with a comparison group and follow-up beyond the end of the program, since we don't yet know whether the gains hold.

Survey results cover completers only, so they likely overstate the average effect for everyone who enrolled.
